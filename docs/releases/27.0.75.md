# 27.0.75 — Less compute overhead and a two-pass Gallery overview

- Single-club plans, grade-cost overviews and overall planning no longer rebuild account context on every internal scoring step. Pure synchronous work uses a local cancellation/surface check; each slice boundary, asynchronous resume and final publication still verifies the account, materials and frozen quotes. Network work keeps its original full context checks. No deadline or quote lifetime is extended.
- The overall reference session first validates each collection's public proposal locally, then separately removes redundant purchases with the original scorer. A complete public scoring lineup can serve as a conservative first-pass witness; external totals are never trusted. Existing owned cards are deducted, but extra owned alternatives may make the later pass cheaper. The complete verification pass is published before refinement, and Stop/Continue retains both validated routes and exact continuations. Temporary scoring memos are released between passes without discarding completed baseline/reference results, avoiding a catalogue-wide accumulation of large scorer caches.
- Route comparisons reuse unchanged ordered cashflow prefixes and compiled immutable proposals within one comparison job. At most eight prefixes are retained, with no GM cache. Selected and displayed routes still use the original full turnover forecast. Three objectives, S/A/B priority, shared exact versions, tax, revolving capital and funding-gap reporting remain unchanged.
- The overview shows verified collection counts, pending refinement and up to 13 unfinished collections with compute/wait/validation timings. The existing bounded diagnostic ring retains only public set IDs, phase enums and numeric timings; names, paths and account identity are excluded. No new storage key or history ring is added.

The latest .74 live log showed successful single-club computations taking 41.7s/30.9s, while frozen local replay took about 1.4s/1.3s. A production-UI regression reproduced 38,691 account-context reads; the new controls need 536/500 while preserving the original cost bounds. This addresses a confirmed source of repeated work, not proof that all live/browser delay had one cause. A 64-collection synthetic differential across three funding levels and three route profiles produced identical complete outputs, including costs and funding, with route CPU roughly 17–42% lower in that control. These are offline controls, not current-account speed or market-optimum guarantees.

Budget, per-card caps, FSU, manual locks, main-squad protection, buy/list/submit paths, journals, pending transactions and historical collection evidence are not cleared or relaxed.
