## Cost objective

Sam explicitly requested whole-plan cost optimization. New native Puzzle plans compare **owned material market value plus additional purchase spend**, instead of pricing owned cards at zero. Additional cash expenditure is still separate and is the only amount passed into the existing purchase plan/limits. This is not an increase in spending authority.

For a feasible owned-only squad, a 20,000-node read-only refinement uses valid public values and retains the original squad as incumbent. A bounded joint comparison can then replace it with a strictly lower total-value route; no better result or a read-only failure retains the feasible owned baseline. Quotes cover at most 250 distinct safe owned versions, prioritizing the incumbent, with a 90-second valuation-read deadline. Missing owned prices are never invented as zero: a baseline with missing selected values is retained with an incomplete-valuation notice, and incomplete/missing/failed/expired owned values switch the joint route back to cash-cost planning with an explicit incomplete-valuation notice, preserving feasible unpriced materials without claiming zero market value. If owned values expire after a route has been found, its feasibility is retained but stale totals and optimality claims are cleared; expired purchase quotes still block. Baseline freshness is checked before comparison, retention and final display. Mixed-route search keeps its existing 20,000/50,000 shared budget and catalog read limits.

The UI separately shows whole material value, existing material value and additional purchases. A synthetic case reduced owned value from 12,000 to 2,200, and another avoids sacrificing a 10,000-valued owned card merely to reduce additional cash. These are offline regressions, not promised live savings. Quotes, limited candidates, search exhaustion and external solution differences mean no global cheapest-squad guarantee.
